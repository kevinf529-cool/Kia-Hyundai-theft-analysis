# Kia-Hyundai-theft-analysis

R-based data analysis and visualization of Kia and Hyundai vehicle theft trends across U.S. cities.

# Kia & Hyundai Vehicle Theft Data Story

## Project Overview

This project analyzes the sharp increase in Kia and Hyundai vehicle thefts across several U.S. cities. Using multiple datasets and R-based visualizations, the analysis examines how theft patterns changed over time, which locations experienced the largest increases, and how significant Kia and Hyundai thefts became within overall vehicle theft trends.

The project was designed as a data story for local governments, law enforcement agencies, vehicle owners, and other stakeholders interested in understanding and responding to the increase in vehicle theft.

## Business Question

How did Kia and Hyundai vehicle thefts change over time, which communities were most affected, and what insights could support targeted theft-prevention efforts?

## Data

The analysis uses four datasets containing information about:

- Monthly Kia and Hyundai thefts in Milwaukee
- Kia and Hyundai theft totals across multiple U.S. cities
- Police agency-level vehicle theft trends
- Monthly theft trends across selected U.S. cities

The datasets were examined and prepared separately before being used to build the visualizations in the final data story.

## Analysis & Visualizations

Six visualizations were created to tell the story from local and national perspectives:

1. Monthly Kia and Hyundai theft trends in Milwaukee
2. Kia and Hyundai vehicles as a percentage of Milwaukee vehicle thefts
3. Comparison of Kia/Hyundai and other vehicle thefts across cities
4. Communities experiencing large increases in vehicle theft
5. Kia and Hyundai's share of reported Milwaukee vehicle thefts
6. Monthly Kia and Hyundai theft trends across selected U.S. cities

Different visualization types were selected based on the question being answered, including line charts, an area chart, stacked bars, a treemap, and a donut chart.

## Key Findings

Kia and Hyundai thefts increased substantially in Milwaukee during the study period and became a significant part of the city's overall vehicle theft problem.

Across the full study period, Kia and Hyundai vehicles represented approximately 48% of reported Milwaukee vehicle thefts.

The problem also extended beyond Milwaukee. Among the selected cities in the national comparison, Denver showed the largest sustained increase, while Portland and Atlanta experienced noticeable increases later in the study period.

The analysis also showed that theft patterns varied considerably between locations, indicating that communities did not experience the problem equally.

## Recommendations

The findings support targeted prevention efforts rather than treating vehicle theft as an identical problem in every location.

Potential responses include:

- Expanding access to anti-theft devices
- Increasing awareness of available software updates
- Providing timely information to affected vehicle owners
- Directing prevention resources toward communities experiencing the largest increases

## Limitations & Ethical Considerations

The project uses publicly available vehicle theft data and does not contain personally identifiable information.

Reporting practices may differ between jurisdictions, so city-level results should be interpreted as observed trends rather than exact comparisons between locations.

Data preprocessing was performed to prepare records for visualization, and records were not intentionally excluded to influence the conclusions.
